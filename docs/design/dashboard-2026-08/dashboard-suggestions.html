I Miss You Memorial Studio · Dashboard Roadmap Suggestions · August 19, 2026

What the dashboard should do next

Thirteen suggestions, prioritized into three tiers, each with a visual example in our system. Sources: the dashboard research, gaps between the live product and the new dashboard, revenue opportunities, and skills we already have built. Everything follows the locked rules: plain language, no pronouns in chrome, no urgency, undo everywhere.

Tier 1

Do now · small effort, immediate value

Gaps where the product already has the feature or the data, and the dashboard just needs the control.

01

Privacy control: public, unlisted, password

The live product already supports all three (it is on the pricing comparison table), but the dashboard has no control for it. Families managing a fresh loss often want unlisted for the first weeks. One segmented control in Share.

Notification settings + a weekly summary email

QR code for the page · print a card for the service

Memorials are physical events. A QR code next to the share link lets families put the page on the program, the flowers card, the table at the reception. The single biggest visitor-acquisition moment is the service itself.

04

A quiet week strip on the Overview

Not analytics — belonging. One mono line: who came, what they left. Counts only ever grow (endowed progress, the one meter the research cleared for grief). No charts, no trends, no comparisons.

The waiting-memory email should carry the memory text and two buttons — Approve to page / Decline — one tap, no login hunt. GoFundMe's conveyor, delivered to the inbox. Highest-leverage version of the approval flow for older users.

Next · bigger builds, clear payoff

Features that deepen the page and sell Plus on their own.

Ask the family · shareable question cards

The wall only grows if people write. Send a question card to the family group chat — one tap opens the page with the question pre-filled. Scaffolded writing lowers the emotional cost (the GoFundMe update-template insight), and it fills the page with real voices.

Tier 3

Later · the long game

Bigger bets that compound: succession, community, and physical keepsakes.

11

Legacy contact · succession for the page itself

Memorial pages outlive their owners. Let the owner name who inherits the page if the account goes quiet. No other feature says "forever" more credibly — it makes the Permanence Pledge structural.

12

Followers · invited back on the anniversary

Visitors who leave a flower can follow the page. Once a year, on the date the family chose to mark, they get one quiet invitation to return. Grief is lighter carried together — and it is a yearly return loop no growth hack could match.

13

The printed book · the page as a keepsake

The Concierge tier already ships a printed keepsake book. Self-serve it: one click turns the page — photos, obituary, the wall — into a hardcover proof to order. The highest-margin product we could attach, and the family already made it.
