Part I

The twelve laws every great dashboard obeys

These are not opinions — they're measured properties of human attention. Every pattern in Part III exists because of one of these.

01 · Hick's Law
More choices, slower decisions
Decision time grows with the number of options offered.
→ A home screen earns 3–5 actions, never 12.
02 · Fitts's Law
Big and close is fast
Time to reach a target depends on its size and distance.
→ The most-used action gets the biggest, nearest button.
03 · Miller's Law
Memory holds 7±2 chunks
People can only hold a handful of things in mind at once.
→ Group content into labeled clusters of 3–5, never a wall of 15 equal numbers.
04 · Jakob's Law
Users live on other sites
People expect your product to work like everything else they use.
→ Put nav, search, and settings where every dashboard puts them; invent in the content, not the chrome.
05 · Tesler's Law
Complexity must live somewhere
Either the designer absorbs complexity or the user does.
→ Smart defaults and pre-computed comparisons — never make the user configure.
06 · Doherty Threshold
Respond under 400ms
Engagement collapses when the system makes people wait.
→ Skeletons and cached numbers instantly; a slow dashboard stops being consulted.
07 · Aesthetic–Usability
Beautiful feels usable
Pleasing designs are perceived as more trustworthy and forgiven more.
→ Aligned grids and a restrained palette buy real trust in the numbers.
08 · Peak–End Rule
Endings are remembered
People judge an experience by its peak and its final moment.
→ Design the "all clear" state deliberately — it's what checking in feels like.
09 · Gestalt Proximity
Nearness means together
Things placed close are read as one group.
→ Whitespace is the grouping tool — gaps instead of boxes inside boxes.
10 · Von Restorff
The different one is seen
Among similar items, only the one that differs gets remembered.
→ Accent color marks exactly one thing per screen; highlight everything, and nothing is.
11 · Data-Ink Ratio (Tufte)
Erase everything that isn't data
Every pixel should carry information or be removed.
→ Strip borders, shadows, and decorative icons before you ever shrink the number.
12 · Information Scent
Links must smell like their destination
People only follow paths that clearly promise their goal.
→ "Read the 3 waiting memories," never "More."
Part II

The anatomy — six zones, in the order people ask

Every good dashboard answers the same questions in the same order: where am I → am I okay → what needs me → where can I go → tell me more → can I rest now. Status always precedes navigation, because people came to check, not to browse.

1 · Identity
"Where am I, and whose is this?"
Account, person, date scope. Every number below is meaningless without it. (Ours: the arch portrait and her name.)
2 · Status
"Am I okay?"
One glanceable answer, above the fold, no scroll. Most visits should be able to end here, satisfied.
3 · Attention
"What needs me?"
Exceptions and decisions, pressing-first. This zone hosts the screen's single primary action — the only zone allowed to speak up.
4 · Navigation
"Where else can I go?"
3–5 high-scent destinations. It ranks below status because navigation is a means, not the goal.
5 · Detail
"Why is it like that?"
Depth behind taps — progressive disclosure gives the few their depth without cluttering the glance for the many.
6 · Reassurance
"Can I trust this, and may I stop?"
Freshness, permanence, a calm close. The peak–end rule says this quiet footer is what the visit feels like afterward. (Ours: the Permanence Pledge.)
Part III

The exemplars — who does it best, and what to take

Grouped by kind. For each: what the home screen is, the signature moves, the principle behind them (in italics), and how it goes mobile.

Operator dashboards — a person tending something they care about (closest to us)
Airbnb HostHospitality

Home is a day-planner, not a report — the "Today" tab orders blocks from most to least pressing.

  • Reservation cards with the suggested action attached ("send check-in message") — context and action colocated kills the tab-shuffle.
  • Five tabs total; pro tools demoted to sub-menus — optimize for the loyal individual, not the power user.
Mobile: app-first, bottom tabs — "everything is inside the app."
GoFundMe OrganizerEmotional stakes · closest cousin

Organizer home = momentum + gratitude queue under high emotion.

  • Thank-you conveyor: thank one donor, the next un-thanked donor auto-appears — one decision at a time becomes a flow state.
  • Scaffolded update composer (photo + news + thanks template) — lower the emotional cost of writing.
  • Comments can be turned off entirely when they turn hostile — the nuclear option is itself a form of care.
Mobile: left menu on desktop becomes bottom menu on mobile.
Shopify AdminCommerce

Home is a task feed: today's numbers strip, then cards like "5 orders to fulfill" that vanish at zero.

  • Rows deep-link straight to the fix — the overview mirrors the merchant's mental model: not "here's data" but "here's what needs you."
  • Only ships metrics proven to trigger a follow-up action — a metric without a next action is decoration.
Mobile: full-parity app, bottom tabs, designed mobile-first.
YouTube StudioBest moderation queue anywhere

The held-for-review queue is the reference design for approval work.

  • One "Held" tab, four verbs per item (approve / remove / report / hide user) — moderation is defaults + exceptions, never a settings matrix.
  • Strictness dial plus a trusted-people allowlist that skips review — set policy once, then only handle edge cases.
Mobile: dedicated Studio app; full moderation from a phone.
Monzo / RevolutBanking

Home = one number (balance) + a row of verbs; five actions max, three taps to anything.

  • Thirty products hidden behind labeled sections — surface frequency, bury depth.
  • Monzo measured "change aversion" and let users snooze a redesign — operators feel redesigns as broken muscle memory; ship change gently.
Mobile: mobile-only by design — the purest thumb-first layouts in the industry.
One Medical (vs MyChart)Healthcare · sensitive context

Bottom nav of exactly four jobs: Home, Appointments, Messages, Records. MyChart is the cautionary tale — what people need buried under institutional structure.

  • Suggested appointment times shown immediately raised bookings 20% — a ready default beats an open search.
  • Single triaged inbox, provider photos for trust — in vulnerable moments, one clear channel beats many.
Mobile: app-first, four bottom tabs.
Etsy Shop ManagerCraft commerce

Leads with "Today's top tasks" before any statistic.

  • Task tabs deep-link into the fix — the dashboard is a router for work, not a destination.
  • Shop Advisor speaks plain-language reminders — the system remembers deadlines so the seller doesn't.
Mobile: separate seller app; the desktop stays the full cockpit.
Squarespace / WixSite owners

Home framed as guidance, not analytics: setup tasks, advice, one clear next step.

  • Dismissible task checklists are the industry standard — operators must own their own to-do list.
  • Empty sections become full-width invitations with one CTA — an empty state is the first task, not a blank.
Mobile: responsive + companion apps; desktop-leaning.
Product & SaaS dashboards — the craft benchmark
StripeFintech

Home = "Today" number row (gross, net, vs yesterday), then mirrored chart cards. Sixty seconds of your morning.

  • Identical chart grammar on every card — when everything is formatted the same, differences become the signal.
  • Every row inspectable down to the event — density is earned only when every row is actionable.
Mobile: scoped companion app (monitoring + quick actions) + home-screen widgets.
LinearSoftware

No metrics home at all — home is an inbox/triage queue.

  • One item, one decision, next — queues minimize decision latency.
  • Empty states are one sentence + one action — the blank screen is peak uncertainty, so teach exactly there.
  • Optimistic UI, 120–180ms motion — perceived speed is trust.
Mobile: native "sidekick" app for away-from-desk triage — not desktop parity.
VercelDeveloper tools

Overview = grid of project cards with live status; header and activity stream deleted "to get to what matters faster."

  • Status visible even in the browser favicon — status you can see without switching focus costs zero attention.
  • Empty state shows the literal command in monospace — instructions beat decoration.
Mobile: responsive web with a floating bottom bar "optimized for one-handed use."
Plausible / FathomAnalytics

The entire product is one page: six KPIs, one chart, panel grid. No sidebar, no report tree.

  • Anti-GA4 by design — eliminating navigation eliminates the recall tax.
  • Click any row to filter the whole page — direct manipulation beats abstract configuration.
Mobile: no app needed — one page just stacks. The one-page bet makes responsive trivial.
Notion HomeWorkspace

A curated widget landing: greeting, recently visited, My Tasks aggregated from everywhere.

  • Aggregation by person, not by container — people think in "my work," not in your information architecture.
  • Barely customizable on purpose — an opinionated home is the guardrail that guarantees a useful default.
Mobile: single column, bottom tabs (Home / Search / Inbox).
MercuryBanking for founders

One huge number — balance + trend — then recent activity. Density calibrated to the founder, not the finance team.

  • Single hero metric — lead with the number that answers the user's first question; the wrong hero signals you don't understand their job.
  • Deliberate friction on big transfers — in high-stakes moments, friction is the trust signal.
Mobile: near parity — a one-metric layout survives the phone unchanged.
Progress & wellbeing dashboards — where the meters live
Apple Fitness RingsHealth

Glance level shows three rings and nothing else — numbers live one layer deeper.

  • A 90%-complete circle begs to be closed — Gestalt closure + the goal-gradient effect (effort accelerates near completion).
  • Daily reset keeps stakes low, deadline firm. Cost: 11:45pm compulsive ring-closing — noted.
Restraint: three goals compressed into one "did I win the day?" state.
Whoop / OuraRecovery

One 0–100 score at 72pt answers "how should I live today?" — charts two taps deeper.

  • Dozens of biometrics compressed into one authoritative number — cognitive simplification: one number is the coaching.
  • Oura's scores explain themselves via named "contributors" — interpretability over raw data. Rest Mode = permission to stop, built in.
Restraint: no streaks, no leaderboards, no raw data up front.
DuolingoLearning · the cautionary king

The streak is the business model: loss aversion on a counter.

  • Works brutally well — 9M+ year-long streaks — loss aversion + sunk cost, the strongest and most dangerous mechanic.
  • Its guilt-trip notifications are the canonical dark pattern; a user quit forever after losing an 850-day streak to a dropped connection.
Lesson for us: import nothing whose engine is loss.
HeadspaceMeditation · the tone teacher

Home is a concierge, not a library: one recommended session by time of day, one big start button.

  • Course bars use unfinished-sequence pull — the Zeigarnik effect, gently.
  • Completion = a 1-second gentle settle, never confetti; 400–800ms transitions physically slow you down — the interface behaves like a considerate presence.
Restraint: no points, no levels, no leaderboards, ever.
Gentler StreakThe anti-streak · most transferable

Progress is a tolerance band, not a target — rest days sit inside the band, so the streak survives rest.

  • "Ill / injured / away" states formally excuse absence — guilt is designed out, autonomy designed in.
  • Compares you only to your own history — never to others.
For us: the closest existing philosophy to a grief-safe meter.
FinchSelf-care · the care-outward insight

You don't maintain your own metric — you tend a creature you love. One progress bar, no leaderboards.

  • Retention beats Duolingo's without a single nag — devotion to something outside yourself outmotivates self-metrics.
  • This is the mechanic for memorials: contributing "for her," never for a score.
For us: the page itself is the tended thing — the wreath, the vigil, the wall.

Part V

The ten universals

The patterns that appeared in nearly every excellent dashboard, across all five industries.

Overview = status + next action, never just data. A dashboard that doesn't propose the next step is a report.
One hero answer above the fold. The home must answer the user's first question without scrolling — most visits should end there, satisfied.
Task feed over chart grid for operators: plain-language sentences the system has already interpreted ("3 memories are waiting"), not metrics to compute.
One decision at a time, auto-advancing. Queues with 2–4 verbs per item (GoFundMe's thank-conveyor, YouTube's Held tab) turn duty into flow.
Empty states teach. One sentence + one action at the moment of maximum uncertainty; an empty operator dashboard is the first task, not a blank.
Progressive disclosure. Show the headline, hide the breakdown behind a tap; depth on demand, never up front.
Consistent card grammar. When every card is formatted identically, the differences become the signal.
Perceived speed is trust. Skeletons over spinners, optimistic UI, under 400ms — a slow dashboard stops being visited.
Motion is rationed. Animation reserved for meaningful moments; motion on information display creates anxiety, not delight.
Mobile is a scoped companion, not shrunken parity. Bottom tabs, thumb-zone actions, glance + unblock; depth stays where the keyboard is.

And the ten mistakes (what "bad dashboard" actually means)

Chart-grid with no hierarchy — nothing is the answer.

Vanity metrics up top — numbers that answer no question.

Everything-equal cards — the anomaly drowns.

Mystery icons without labels — people won't tap what they can't predict.

Infinite settings — the designer's complexity, pushed onto the user.

Scrolling to learn "am I okay?" — the one unforgivable fold violation.

Gauges, 3D, decoration — chartjunk slows comprehension.

Numbers without comparison — a raw figure can't be judged.

False precision — "$3,848,305.93" where "$3.85M" belongs.

Slow filters, blank loads — trains people to stop exploring.

Part VI

The grief lens — what crosses over, what never may

Our caretakers are grieving. The research is unusually clear about which mechanics would wound here, and which translate into care. In grief, restraint is the feature.

Never import

  • Streaks & daily resets — loss aversion weaponizes the exact emotion they're carrying. "A broken streak of visiting her page" is cruelty rendered in UI.
  • Urgency & countdowns — grief has no deadline; time pressure implies mourning can be done wrong or late.
  • Comparison & leaderboards — comparing whose tribute is fuller invites shame.
  • Guilt-toned re-engagement — never "it's been three days since you visited Mom's page."
  • Confetti & variable rewards — slot-machine dopamine feels obscene against loss; a gentle settle, never sparks.

Translates gently

  • Quiet cumulative counts — "127 memories · 34 people" only ever grows, never breaks. Already ours.
  • Completeness as care — "her page is nearly whole," pre-seeded with what already exists so a family starts at 40%, never zero; the unfinished part reads as invitation, not debt.
  • Care directed outward (Finch's insight) — tending something beloved outmotivates any self-metric; the wreath is the tended thing.
  • Permission structures (Gentler Streak) — away states without penalty; return greeted with what arrived, never what was missed: "welcome back — three new memories were added while you were away."
  • Peak–end sessions — end every visit on her: a photo, a line from a memory, not an interface.
  • Slow motion, warm surfaces (Headspace) — 400–800ms easing; the interface as a considerate presence in the room.
Part VII

Where the Study stands, and four directions to choose

First, honestly: measured against all of the above, the current Study already gets a lot right — and has clear gaps. Then four directions, each a coherent whole. Pick one; we break down every part of it together next.

✓ Already aligned with the research

  • Plain-language sentence cards — the operator universal, already our voice.
  • Inline decide — one memory, two verbs, right on the overview.
  • Quiet counts — flowers and candles as truthful accumulation.
  • No streaks, no urgency, no comparison — the grief lens holds.
  • Reassurance zone — the plan strip's "always" is a peak-end close.

✕ Gaps the research exposes

  • No single status answer above the fold — "am I okay?" takes reading eight cards.
  • No auto-advancing queue — deciding memory 2 of 3 means a page change; GoFundMe's conveyor is the fix.
  • Cards are everything-equal — waiting, pictures, story, anniversary all same weight; the anomaly drowns.
  • Mobile nav is a hamburger — bottom tabs win for frequent, repeated visits.
  • No completeness meter — endowed progress framed as care is our biggest unused gentle mechanic.

The four directions

Direction A · Headspace × One Medical

The Quiet Concierge

One recommended act per visit · four bottom tabs · everything else recedes
┌────────────────────────────┐ │ her portrait · her name │ │ │ │ "Tonight, one thing: │ │ a memory from Miriam │ │ is waiting to be read." │ │ [ Read it ] │ │ │ │ everything else, quiet ↓ │ └────────────────────────────┘ [Home] [Waiting] [Pictures] [Her]
The home offers exactly one thing, chosen for you — like Headspace's single session, One Medical's ready appointment. All other zones collapse to whispers. Why it could win: the gentlest possible posture; Hick's law taken to its logical end; perfect for overwhelmed, older, grieving visitors on phones. Risk: families with active pages (many pending memories, multiple tributes) may feel the concierge is hiding their desk from them.
Direction B · GoFundMe × Shopify × YouTube Studio

The Sharpened Desk

Evolution, not revolution · status sentence up top · one-decision conveyor
┌────────────────────────────┐ │ "Her page is tended. │ │ 3 memories wait." ← status│ ├────────────────────────────┤ │ MEMORY 1 of 3 · Miriam │ │ "She kept a chair..." │ │ [Share on her page] [Keep] │ │ → next appears in place │ ├────────────────────────────┤ │ pictures · story · the day │ │ (quiet, smaller, grouped) │ └────────────────────────────┘
Keep today's sentence-card soul, fix the four gaps: a one-line status verdict above the fold, the waiting queue becomes an auto-advancing conveyor (decide → the next memory rises in place, GoFundMe-style), secondary cards shrink into a grouped shelf so the one thing that needs her is the only loud thing. Trusted-people allowlist later, YouTube-style. Why it could win: highest value for lowest risk — it's our desk, sharpened by everything the research proved. Risk: least dramatic; if you want a new room, this is a better-lit version of the old one.
Direction C · Monzo × Whoop × Mercury

The Hearth — one warm number

A single tended-state hero · verbs beneath · depth on demand
┌────────────────────────────┐ │ ( her portrait ) │ │ │ │ Her page is tended. │ │ 341 flowers · 127 candles│ │ 3 memories wait for you │ │ │ │ [Read them] [Add] [Visit] │ ├────────────────────────────┤ │ tap anything to go deeper │ └────────────────────────────┘
One hero state — "tended / waiting for you / resting" — plays the role of Monzo's balance and Whoop's score: the whole page compressed into a word a grieving person can absorb in one breath, with three verbs beneath. Everything else is disclosure. Why it could win: the strongest possible answer to "am I okay?"; radically calm; survives the phone untouched. Risk: a computed "state" for a memorial must never feel like the app grading their grief — the vocabulary needs extreme care.
Direction D · Plausible × Apple's restraint

One Page, no doors

Zero navigation · everything on one calm scroll · sections as rooms
┌────────────────────────────┐ │ her name · status sentence │ │ ── what waits ── │ │ ── what people remember ── │ │ ── the pictures ── │ │ ── her story ── │ │ ── the vigil ── │ │ ── that day, gently ── │ │ ── it stays, always ── │ └────────────────────────────┘
Delete the sidebar entirely. The whole caretaker experience is one scrolling page with labeled rooms — Plausible's bet that eliminating navigation eliminates the recall tax, and NN/g's layer-cake scanning made literal. Editing happens inline, in place. Why it could win: zero learning curve, zero getting lost — the structure a 70-year-old widow never has to think about; trivially responsive. Risk: billing/account chores must still live somewhere discreet, and very full pages make the scroll long — needs a sticky "what waits" anchor.
