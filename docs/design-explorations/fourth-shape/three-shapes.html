why it's shaped this way
  • Voices sit directly under her name — your #1 orbit. The first scroll lands on Sarah's words, not on a feature.
  • Two doors, honest counts. "All ten memories," "35 photographs" — depth is felt, never dumped. This is the pattern older users navigate best: recognition, not exploration.
  • One crescendo: her face → her people → her life → do something → held forever. The end note is the pledge (peak-end rule).
  • "Leave a memory" is on the first screen-and-a-half, not buried at the bottom.
what it wins
  • Fastest comprehension — nothing to learn
  • Gentlest on grief-load and older hands
  • The glance posture is perfect: 30 seconds gives the whole story
  • Easiest to keep beautiful — few surfaces to hold
what it costs
  • Least alive — return-tomorrow is weakest of the three
  • Wall of love is behind a door, so "so many loved her" is told by counts, not felt on the surface
  • Doors must be irresistible or depth goes unseen

The Vigil — presence-first

The intention: the page as a room where the vigil never ends. A compact ceiling holds her name and the living counts; beneath it, one stream interleaves the newest words, photos, and voices — and you can see, truthfully, that others are sitting with her right now. Designed for the return: every day the stream has something new at the top.

why it's shaped this way
  • The stream is the voices — words, photos, and voice notes interleave as one river of love, newest first. Your #1 and #2 orbits share the same surface.

  • The ceiling never leaves — her name and counts stay gently pinned, so the ceremony holds even mid-stream.

what it wins
  • Strongest return-tomorrow of the three — the page is alive
  • "So many people loved her" is felt live, not counted
  • Collaborative by nature — the family watches it together, apart
  • Contribution is always one thumb-tap away
what it costs
  • Feed-gravity is real — one wrong detail and it feels like social media at a funeral
  • Needs activity: quiet pages must degrade gracefully to a Portrait-like stillness
  • Chronology fights curation — the best things sink over time without "most loved" surfacing
  • Most complex to hold to the dignity bar
