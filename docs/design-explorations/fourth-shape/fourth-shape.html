what direction d is
  • Her face is the header. Full-bleed square, name and dates resting on the photograph in bold cream — the strongest possible "I could really see her again."
  • The service card is unmissable — bordered, bold, one Share button. The one errand, solved in the first screen.
  • Four button-tabs, pinned under the hero. Raised edges and a shadow say "press me"; the pressed tab goes dark. Learned once, remembered forever.
  • Memories opens first (your orbit) with "On this day" cycling through dated photographs and people's words — taller now, with dots showing there's more.
  • Likes and comments stay on the cards, exactly where they were. The heart is the page's only gesture — universal, honest, quiet.
  • "Leave a memory" never leaves the thumb.
renaming "add to her vigil"

Candidates, warmest first: "Leave a memory" (chosen — it's what people actually do), "Add to her page," "Share something of hers," "For the family." The word "vigil" was doing brand poetry; the button should do plain kindness.

05 · what you might be missing

The quiet gaps

the gesture gap · decidedVisits are the number

"12,438 people have visited her page." It grows on its own — every visit counts, nobody owes a gesture, and the family watches the number rise like the crowd at the door. The heart stays on individual memories only.

photo dates"On this day" needs honest dates

Many photos carry EXIF dates; scans don't. The design needs graceful language for uncertainty — "around 1988" and "from her photographs" — and a one-tap way for family to place undated photos on the timeline. Never guess precisely; grief punishes wrong dates.

older eyes, one more stepA text-size control that's findable

The accessibility widget exists, but bottom-left corners go unseen. For this audience, "Aa" belongs in the header line — one tap, three sizes, remembered. Same for a "pause motion" that actually stops the Today cycle.

the empty pageDay one has no memories yet

Every direction above assumes ten memories. The night the page is made, it has zero. The empty state is the family's first impression: her photo, her name, and one warm line inviting the first memory — designed, not defaulted.

the service week vs. foreverThe page has two lives

Weeks one and two are about the service and the gathering; year two is about "on this day." The service card should quietly step back after the date passes — the design should breathe with time, not stay frozen at the funeral.

trust at the doorModeration, visible but soft

"Every word waits for the family before it appears" is why grandparents feel safe posting. One quiet line under the Leave-a-memory button carries it. Safety signals are design, not copy.
