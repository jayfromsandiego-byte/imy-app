I Miss You Memorial · internal design brief · august 2026

The shape of remembering

A first-principles look at what a tribute page has to do, the psychology underneath it, an honest audit of where Eleanor's page stands, and three structural directions — before we choose anything.

01 · first principles

What is this page, actually?

Strip away every feature and a tribute page has five jobs. Every design decision should serve one of them — anything that serves none is ornament, and ornament competes with her.

job 1Recognize"That's her." Her face, her name, instantly — the whole reason the page exists.
job 2FeelSit with her for a moment. One thing at a time, at the visitor's own pace.
job 3ContributeLay a flower, light a candle, leave a memory — in under a minute, without an account.
job 4CoordinateThe service. Date, place, share — the one practical errand, always findable.
job 5ReturnA reason to come back tomorrow. Something new to see; someone new was here.

And it serves three postures: the glance (30 seconds, from a text-message link, probably standing), the sit (five quiet minutes, often at night), and the add (someone with something to give). The page today mostly serves the sit. The glance and the add have to work harder than they should.

02 · the factors

What a designer weighs here

grieving cognition

Grief measurably reduces working memory and attention. The research is consistent: less text, fewer simultaneous choices, one idea per screen. A grieving visitor doesn't scan — they drift. The design must be navigable by drifting.

older hands, older eyes

Many visitors are 65+, on phones. That means:

  • Touch targets ≥ 44px, generously spaced
  • No hover-only reveals — hover is a desktop luxury; every caption needs a tap path
  • No drags, no sliders, no pinch — taps and vertical scroll only
  • Body text ≥ 17px, real contrast on every surface
one canvas, every screen

Design the phone column first; let desktop be a wider, calmer version of the same column. Horizontal patterns (rails, rivers, side-by-side splits) are desktop thinking — on mobile they become hidden content and accidental swipes. Everything important lives in the vertical scroll.

universal, not neutral-gray

The page must hold a grandmother, a father, a young man, a wife. Universality comes from structure, not from stripping warmth: the person's own photos carry their character; the frame stays quiet and lets them color it. One family of surfaces, one accent, zero themed ornament.

motion discipline

Motion should mean something is alive (a count rising, a new memory arriving) — never that the page is performing. Nothing moves that the visitor didn't ask to move, except one heartbeat surface, clearly pausable. Auto-rotation fights reading and older reflexes.

trust & permanence

Families are handing us the most precious thing they have. The design signals permanence through stillness, weight, and consistency — the same care on every surface, no seams where the "product" shows through the keepsake.

03 · the psychology

Why things sit where they sit

Center = ceremony
Left = readingalignment carries meaning

Centered composition reads as formal, held, monumental — right for her name, her portrait, the count of flowers. Left-aligned reads as human and tellable — right for stories, memories, anything longer than a line. The page should use exactly these two alignments, each with one meaning. Mixing them per-section (as we do now) is why it feels less composed than it is.

First and lastserial position effect

People remember the first thing and the last thing. First: her face and name — never a feature. Last: the permanence pledge — the feeling that this is held. Everything commercial belongs in the middle-shadow, quiet, or off the tribute entirely.

One door per depthprogressive disclosure

The calm products never show the archive — they show a taste and one labeled door ("35 photographs →"). Depth behind doors is felt as richness; depth on the page is felt as weight. Our page currently opens every door at once, which is why it runs nine screens long.

Recognition over recallhow older users navigate

A visitor should never have to remember where something was. A short, always-visible map (3–5 words) beats a long scroll of surprises. Sections need stable, predictable shapes — same header pattern, same rhythm — so the second visit feels like a place they know.

The visible countsocial proof of love

"2,168 flowers" does more emotional work than any paragraph — it's the crowd at the funeral, countable. Contribution numbers (flowers, candles, memories) are the page's honest heartbeat and deserve prime placement; they also invite the next contribution better than any button copy.

Together, apartambient co-presence

Grieving happens alone at 2am and together on the anniversary. A quiet presence signal — "three people are sitting with her now," a flower landing as you watch — turns a page into a vigil without a single chat feature. Presence is the collaborative feature; it just has to be truthful.

The peak and the endemotional pacing

A visit should have a shape: arrive (her face) → warm (her life) → peak (the voices of people who loved her) → settle (add your own, see the service) → close (held forever). One emotional crescendo, not nine equal sections shouting evenly.

04 · the audit

Eleanor's page, honestly

what's true and working
  • The rituals are real. Flowers, candles, a truthful count — no other memorial product has an honest heartbeat.
  • The Stone + wreath is a genuinely ownable identity — recognizable at thumbnail size.
  • Memory cards carry voice. Name, relation, words, hearts — the ingredients are right.
  • The brand type system (Besley + mono details) is warm and distinct without being gendered.
  • The accessibility widget and pronoun care are ahead of the category.
what's fighting us
  • Nine screens of equal loudness. No crescendo, no doors — every archive fully open on the page.
  • Three photo systems (gallery grid, garden river, memory snapshots) doing one job with three different manners.
  • Layered ornament — pressed flowers + tape + torn paper + scrapbook + leaves. Each is lovely; together they're the papier-mâché we're scrapping.
  • Alignment drift. Centered, left, and justified sections alternate without a rule.
  • Hover-dependent reveals and horizontal patterns that mobile can't honor.
  • The add-a-memory door is buried at the bottom of the longest section.
05 · the landscape

Where the good answers already live

Nobody has designed this page perfectly — but pieces of it are solved in adjacent worlds. Steal the bones, not the skin:

NYT · Guardian obituariesThe editorial column

One measured column, one portrait treatment, ceremonial name, restrained rule lines. Proof that dignity is a layout, not a decoration.

Apple & Google Photos memoriesOne thing today

The archive greets you with a single chosen moment. A thousand photos behind one card — the strongest anti-overwhelm pattern there is, and the reason to return daily.

Zola · Joy wedding sitesThe life-event page

The closest structural cousin: one person-pair, one event block, many contributors, guest photo albums, zero-account participation. Their info architecture is battle-tested on every generation of relative.

Unsplash · FlickrThe index and the room

A quiet justified grid as index; tap opens a dark room — one photo, whispered caption, swipe. Browsing and beholding as two separate postures.

9/11 Memorial · Vietnam WallNames as monument

The most moving memorials are typography and count, almost nothing else. Restraint reads as respect; the count reads as love made visible.

Aesop · Le LaboOne accent, many rooms

A single surface family and a single accent color holding wildly different content together. Cohesion is repetition of a few decisions, not variety of many.

06 · three shapes

Structural directions, sketched

Same brand, same content, three different skeletons. Wireframes only — tone comes later. Each is drawn phone-first; desktop is the same column, wider and calmer.

direction a

The Portrait — one ceremonial column

Five screens, one crescendo. Everything present, every archive behind one labeled door. The compact answer: a visitor can hold the whole page in their head after one visit.

phone · 5 screens total
desktop · same column, wider
photographywordsritual / contributepledge
direction b

The Album — a cover and four rooms

A short ceremonial cover (portrait, name, rituals, service), then four stable tabs: Her life · Photos · Memories · Service. Nothing is long; everything is one tap away. The app-like answer — strongest for older visitors who revisit, because rooms never move.

phone · tabs stay pinned
desktop · rooms, not scroll
photographywords / tabsritual / contributepledge
direction c

The Vigil — presence-first

Her portrait and the living counts stay gently pinned while one stream flows beneath: today's chosen moment, then the newest memories, photos, and voices interleaved as one chronological river of love. Presence is ambient — you see flowers land, you see who's sitting with her. The collaborative answer; the strongest reason to return.
