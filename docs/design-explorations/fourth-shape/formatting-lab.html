Three problems, fifteen answers

Every variation below is live at miniature scale — tap, scroll, and hover them. The ★ marks where I'd put my money, but each card carries its honest trade so we can decide together.

problem a · her life

Eight chapters, not four

A full life easily carries 6–10 chapters. Here's the same rail populated with eight, formatted five ways.

a1

The arrow rail

★ my pick

The rail keeps its golden line, and honest ‹ › arrows page it one screenful at a time — the same manners as the memory arrows, so nothing new to learn. Edges fade to whisper "there's more."

winsScales to any number of chapters · arrows are visible, tappable, learned once
costsLater chapters live "offstage" — the fade must be obvious enough to invite the arrow
a2

The bookshelf

No scrolling at all: chapters wrap into a quiet grid, every era visible at once like spines on a shelf. Tap one, the book opens below.

winsZero hidden content — the whole life in one glance · calmest for older visitors
costsTakes vertical room as chapters grow · loses the left-to-right "journey" feeling you love
a3

The decade doors

Decade pills above the rail — 1940s · 1970s · 1990s · 2010s. Tap a decade and the rail glides to those chapters. Arrows still work; decades are the express lane.

winsFast travel across a long life · reads like a timeline index
costsTwo navigation layers to understand — slightly more to learn
a4

The stations line

The chapters compress to stations on one golden line — era below, title above, no cards at all. Tap a station, the one big chapter card opens beneath. Maximum left-to-right, minimum furniture.

winsThe purest timeline — the whole life is literally one line · elegant at any count
costsStations get tight past ~10 chapters · small tap targets need care on touch
a5

The table of contents

Chapters as a vertical contents column — era, title, expandable years — with the photograph answering on the right. Reads like the front of a book; nothing moves sideways at all.

winsInfinitely scalable · screen-reader and keyboard friendly by nature
costsAbandons the horizontal journey entirely · tall with many chapters open

Three at a time

The stream becomes pages: exactly three full cards, big honest arrows, dots that promise a finite amount. Chips filter; pages never scroll. Tap the arrows — they work.

winsFully finite, fully calm — the Netflix row grammar older visitors already know

b4

The index and the reading chair

Every memory's title and author in a quiet list on the left — the whole wall scannable in five seconds. Tap one; it opens fully in the reading pane with its snapshot, story, hearts, and comments. Tap the list — it works.

winsTen or a hundred memories, same calm — the archive pattern that never overwhelms
costsLess emotional at first sight — titles must carry weight before any photo is seen
b5

The quilt

Every memory as a small square — title, one line, author — stitched into one quilt you can take in whole. Tap a square and it blooms into the full card. No scroll, no pages: one fabric of love.

wins"So many people loved her," visible as a single object · beautiful when the wall grows
costsPhotos demoted to the open state · squares tempt skimming over reading
