I Miss You Memorial
The letter audit · august 2026

Eighteen screens, held to first principles.

The letter is the highest-friction moment in the whole product. Every screen below is graded against one test: does it make expressing everything easier — or just shorter?

Part I

The grammar of a screen.

Every screen speaks in the same order, and every element has exactly one job. This is the desktop-first anatomy.

Eyebrow
Part III · Their story
Where am I. Gold small caps — orientation, never urgency.
Header
Tell us a little of Eleanor’s story.
The question itself. Nine words or fewer, their name inside it, one italic emphasis — the emotional word.
Subtext
In your own words. A few sentences are enough.
One sentence, one job: permission or clarification. Never both, never two lines.
Why-line
why we ask · these words open her page
Only on asks that could raise doubt: photo, obituary, voice, email. A whisper, not a disclaimer.
Input
She was the first one awake in every house…
One input type per screen. White field, warm focus glow, autofocused on desktop.
Nav
← back  Continue  press enter ↵  not yet · skip
One primary. Back is quiet, skip is honest and never buried, Enter always works.
Whisper
saved · come back anytime
The safety net, said out loud. Losing an answer is the one unforgivable failure.

Question by question.

Verdicts: keep as is · tune the wording or input · rework the mechanics.

What was their name?keep
The perfect opener — easiest possible ask, instant momentum, and it powers the name echo everywhere after. Autofocus + Enter already work.
02
Who was Eleanor to you?tune
Right position, right input. Tune: selecting a chip should show a soft check and let Enter advance immediately — one click, one keystroke, done. Keep "or in your own words" visible, not hidden behind a toggle.
03

Warm, skippable, kind skip copy ("they didn't · skip"). The name echo makes it personal. Nothing to fix.

Part III · their story
08
Tell us a little of Eleanor’s story.rework
The heaviest required ask — the blank page, mid-letter. Rework: three quiet starter chips above the field ("how you met her" · "what she was known for" · "an ordinary morning with her") that drop a sentence-opener into the textarea; a taller field that grows as they write; "even one sentence is enough" as the error state instead of a red demand. Phase 2: a speak-instead button.why we ask · these words open her page
09
The obituary, if you would like one.tune
Most families paste, not write. Tune: lead with two choices — "paste one you have" / "skip freely" — and only open the big field on the first. The sub already grants permission beautifully.
10
The small things that were theirs.keep
A phrase and a song — two short fields, both optional, high tribute-value per keystroke. This is the question people thank you for.
11
A voice you want to keep.keep
Plus-tagged honestly, skip is easy, "kept safe either way" is the right promise. Leave it be.
12
A few moments from Eleanor’s life.tune
Tune: seed three empty rows with era hints ("as a child · a year you guess is fine" / "in their prime" / "a recent year"), Enter adds the next row, and a year alone is a valid moment. Guessed years should feel welcome — precision is the enemy of memory here.
Part IV · what they loved
13
What did they love?keep
The payoff mechanic is already in the sub — each love becomes a space on the page, and the design follows. The tiles are now clean white cards. This screen sells the product mid-letter; touch nothing.
Part V · their page
14
Photos and videos, so the page feels like them.tune
Tune: drag-and-drop the whole desktop folder selection at once; thumbnails render as small keepsake frames while uploads run in the background — never make them watch a spinner. The 12-photo free line is honest; keep it in the sub, not as an interruption.
15
Where their page will live.keep
The address with a live preview. Default stays chosen-for-them with "choose how it reads" for those who care — the right split of effort.
16
Is there a service planned?keep
"If plans are still forming, skip this" — permission exactly where panic lives. Keep.
17
Where should we send their page?tune
The email ask, near the end — correct (value first, account never). Tune: add the trust whisper.why we ask · only to send her page · never public, never shared
18
Everything is ready.rework
One-per-screen's known weakness is revision. Rework: before the seal, a quiet review list — every answer in one column, each with a small "change" link that jumps back and returns. Then the stone fills as the page is made — the filling-stone moment we reserved for exactly here.
Part III

The friction work, desktop first.

What makes eighteen screens feel like ten minutes — without ever cutting what a family needs to say.

1

The whole letter answers to the keyboard — autofocus every field, Enter commits, number keys pick chips, Shift+Enter breaks lines in stories.

2

The thread gilds fast early — six quick facts fill a third of it in the first two minutes; momentum before the heavy middle.

3

Effort lives where love is — required: name, relationship, years, one story, email. Everything else skippable, and skipping is styled with dignity, not shame.

4

Starter chips beat blank pages — every long-text screen offers three quiet openers; typing begins as finishing a sentence, not starting one.

5

The page previews as it forms — the photo lands in the arch on screen seven, the address reads live on fifteen, the stone fills at the seal.

6

A breath before Part III — one interstitial line ("take your time · everything is saved") before the story chapter, the letter's steepest hill.

7

Review before the seal — every answer, one column, one "change" link each; nobody re-walks eighteen screens to fix a typo.

8

No surprises, ever — the count never grows, the thread never retreats, uploads never block, and nothing entered is ever lost.
